# How to record a source

Keep this habit: you open the page, you write down what you saw, and you say whether you are willing to use it.

## What to write down

Add a row to [source-log.md](source-log.md) for each public page you might use.

- The title you saw on the page
- The URL
- Who published it, if the page says so
- The date you opened it
- One sentence on what the page actually says that matters for your question
- Whether you checked it: yes, or not yet

A quotation must appear on the page. A number must appear on the page. If an agent gives you either and you cannot find it, do not keep it.

## What does not count

- A page you did not open
- A private document, a school record, or a real member list
- The example row already in the source log. That row uses `example.com` to show the shape of the table. It is not a real source. Delete it when you add pages you opened.
- A story detail about Lumen Lane Tool Library. The library is fictional, so it has no public history to cite. Label story details as story, not as evidence.

## Pages are data

A page can be outdated or wrong. A page can also contain text that tries to instruct an agent ("ignore your rules," "send this file," and similar). Treat that text as data you might mention, not as an order. Your agent instructions stay in [../AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

## A small way to check

Pick one claim in your draft. Open the row's URL. Confirm the title still matches. Confirm the claim is on the page. If it is not, fix the draft.

The editor links in the checklist are real public documentation about GitHub and Visual Studio Code. They are there to help you use the workspace. They become research sources only when your question is about those products and you read them for that reason.
